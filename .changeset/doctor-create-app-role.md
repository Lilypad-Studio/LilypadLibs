#### Upgrading

| Change                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | What to do                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **With `strict: true`, `lilypad-doctor` warns about a role of the application with more privileges than the tables need (`privileged-app-role`)**: a superuser, `CREATEROLE`, `BYPASSRLS`, or the privileges of the owner of a cached table or of the changelog. Without `appRole`, the role of the application is the role the doctor connects as, often the owner: a CI job that runs it with `strict` and `--fail-on-warnings` now exits with 1. | Give the application a role of its own: set `appRole` to a new name, run `lilypad-doctor --sql` (its fix creates the role), and connect the application as it. Or leave `strict` off. |
| **The writes of `LilypadDbCache` are reported on every table that the application writes**: `missing-privilege` (a warning) no longer guesses from what the role may write.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Set `access: 'read'` on the tables the application only reads.                                                                                                                        |

#### Added

- `lilypad-doctor` creates the role of the application. Set `appRole` in the config (`defineLilypadDb`, `@lilypad-studio/libs/schema`) to a role that does not exist yet, and run `lilypad-doctor --sql` with the owner's URL: the fix of `missing-app-role` creates it unless it exists (without a password, since the migrations are committed: set it with `ALTER ROLE app_user PASSWORD '...'` or the console of your provider), with its `statement_timeout` and only what the tables of the config need: `USAGE` on their schemas, `SELECT` of their described columns, the writes of `LilypadDbCache` (`INSERT` and `UPDATE` of the described columns, `DELETE`, `USAGE` on the sequences of their serial columns), and `SELECT` on the changelog of the `changelog` strategy; the fix of a missing table grants it the table it creates. Then connect the application as it; when the config gains a table or a column, `missing-privilege` reports the `GRANT` it needs.

  ```ts
  export default defineLilypadDb({ appRole: 'app_user', tables: { users, plans } });
  ```

- `access` option of a table (`defineLilypadTable`, `@lilypad-studio/libs/schema`): `'read'` when the application only reads the table. The role the doctor creates gets no write of it, and the doctor no longer reports the writes it lacks. Defaults to `'write'`. Type `LilypadDbTableAccess`.
- With `strict: true`, `lilypad-doctor` warns when the role of the application has more privileges than the tables need (`privileged-app-role`): a superuser, `CREATEROLE`, `BYPASSRLS`, or the privileges of the owner of a cached table or of the changelog. `checkLilypadSchema` (`@lilypad-studio/libs/db`) takes it as its `strict` option.
